For families

Protecting your child shouldn't mean identifying your child.

Set Guardian up once, then approve the apps your child uses with a tap. No app needs their name, birthday, face, or exact age. Guardian gives platforms the answer they need, not your child's identity.

Linking your family

Prove it once. We keep nothing.

Verification is a one-time ceremony with a simple rule: the proof does its job, then it disappears.

  1. 1

    Show it's you

    A one-time ID check with our verification partner confirms you're a real adult. About two minutes, once, ever.

    you: verified

  2. 2

    Prove you're the parent

    Your birth certificate or guardianship document goes straight to the verification partner. It never touches Guardian's servers; we keep the answer, never the details.

    the answer: yes

  3. 3

    The paperwork disappears

    The moment it checks out, the document is destroyed. No copy with us, no copy with the partner. What survives is the result: you are this child's parent.

    the document: gone

  4. 4

    A private trust credential takes its place

    From then on, one private trust credential connects you and your kid. When an app asks, it answers with your approval and an age range, and every app sees a different code, so no two apps can compare notes about your child.

    the credential: yours

Questions parents actually ask

Straight answers. Where we're not finished yet, we say so.

What does the platform learn about my kid?

Two things: an age range (like “9–12”) and your answer. Not a name, not a birthday, not a photo, not an email address. If a platform asks us for more, the answer is no.

What do you keep?

Your approvals (the list you see in the app) plus what we need to honor them: your verified parent status and each kid's age band. We don't see what your kid does inside an app, who they talk to, or where they are. We carry your answer to the door; we don't follow anyone through it.

What if I change my mind?

Tap the approval, turn it off. The platform's receipt goes dead the next time they check it. There's no waiting period and nobody to argue with.

What about my other kids?

Each kid gets their own profile, own age band, own asks. A yes for Emma is never a yes for Leo, and platforms can't even tell your kids are related.

Is this legally recognized consent?

That's the goal, and we won't fudge it: US law (COPPA) requires platforms to get a parent's verifiable consent, and Guardian is being built to meet that bar. We're pre-launch: until our legal review is finished and published, treat this as a preview, not a promise.

What does it cost?

The plan: free for families; platforms pay us. We're pre-launch, so pricing isn't final, but “charge parents for their kid's privacy” is not on the table. If anything changes, the waitlist hears it first.
